# Macro backdrop — 2026-10-24

Source: FRED, public CSV endpoint. `official_stat`. Checked deliberately this session rather
than discovered by accident, per the 2026-10-12 lesson.

| Series | 11 Sep | 12 Oct | **24 Oct** | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10-year Treasury | 4.83% | 5.22% | **4.96%** | ↘ eased back, −26bp from peak |
| Fed funds | 3.63% | 3.75% | **3.75%** | flat after the hike |

## The two signals are now pointing in opposite directions

**Good news for valuations.** The 10-year has retreated 26bp from its October peak. At 4.96%
the implied discount rate (holding my 5.2-point equity risk premium) is about **10.2%**, against
the 10.0% my DCFs assumed — materially less punishing than the 10.4% implied three weeks ago.
Fair values are roughly 2–3% below my original assumption rather than 5.3%.

**Bad news for the businesses.** Three separate measures of economic health are deteriorating
together, which is more informative than any one of them moving:

- **Credit spreads have widened 69bp since September** and have risen at every checkpoint. This
  is the measure that usually moves before earnings do — lenders repricing risk.
- **Consumer sentiment has fallen 7.1 points in six weeks**, from 55.2 to 48.1. That is not
  noise; it is a trend across three readings.
- **Unemployment has ticked up** for the first time in this series, 4.10% → 4.20%.

## What this means for existing positions

**It validates the Booking concern specifically.** My BKNG thesis named a consumer recession as
the bear case and that case is down 13.6% and running. Falling sentiment, rising unemployment
and widening credit are all the mechanism that bear case described. The thesis is not yet
broken, but the environment is moving toward it rather than away.

**It argues against adding consumer-discretionary or economically-sensitive exposure now.**
Amphenol (AI capital spending) and anything tied to discretionary consumption are facing a
deteriorating backdrop, whatever their company-specific merits.

**It argues for non-discretionary demand** — businesses whose customers cannot easily defer
purchase: healthcare, essential services, contractual/regulated revenue, replacement cycles.

## What it does NOT tell me

Nothing about which individual stock is mispriced. Macro sets the backdrop; it does not pick
stocks. A deteriorating consumer does not make a cheap stock cheaper, and tight-but-widening
credit spreads at 3.40% are still historically low — there is no distress on offer.

Also worth stating: three readings is a short series. Sentiment can rebound; one unemployment
tick is not a trend. I am treating this as "lean away from cyclicals", not "a recession is
coming", because I have no basis for the second claim.
