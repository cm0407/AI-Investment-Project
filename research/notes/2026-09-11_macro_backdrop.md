## What this means for the ideas in this session

**1. The discount rate assumption is defensible but not generous.** The system's DCF uses a
10% required return against a 4.83% risk-free rate — an equity risk premium of about 5.2
points. That is reasonable. If the 10-year had been at 3%, every valuation here would look
better, and it is worth remembering that none of these theses need rates to fall.

**2. Credit stress is absent, and that cuts both ways.** A high-yield spread of 2.71% is
historically very tight. Nothing is breaking. But tight spreads also mean **the market is
not offering much compensation for risk anywhere** — there are no distressed bargains on
offer, which is consistent with a screen that found few genuinely cheap high-quality names.

**3. The consumer is the soft spot.** Sentiment at 55.2 is weak by historical standards even
after improving. Any thesis that depends on discretionary consumer spending accelerating is
leaning against this reading. Unemployment at 4.1% and continued job growth argue the other
way. Net: the consumer is not collapsing, but it is not a tailwind either.

**4. The curve is no longer inverted** (+0.39), removing one recession signal that was
flashing in earlier years.

**Bottom line for stock selection:** this is a mid-to-late-cycle backdrop with tight credit,
restrictive mortgage rates, a soft-but-stable consumer and a normal yield curve. It argues
for businesses whose earnings do not depend on a consumer re-acceleration or on falling
rates — recurring revenue, contractual or non-discretionary demand — and against
cyclicals bought on a hoped-for recovery.

**What it does NOT tell me:** anything about which individual stock is mispriced. Macro
sets the backdrop; it does not pick stocks.
