## 1. The business in one paragraph

Ulta Beauty is the largest speciality beauty retailer in the United States, selling both mass
and prestige cosmetics, skincare and fragrance in roughly 1,400 stores, most with an in-store
salon. Its distinguishing features are carrying budget and luxury brands side by side, and a
very large loyalty programme.

### ⚠️ The most important thing in this document

**Revenue grew ~10% in the latest year while operating income FELL.**

Read the last three rows carefully:

- Revenue: $11.21B → $11.30B → **$12.39B** (rising)
- Operating income: $1.68B → $1.56B → **$1.53B** (falling)
- Net income: $1.29B → $1.20B → **$1.15B** (falling — **down 11% over three years**)
- Diluted EPS: $26.03 → $25.34 → **$25.64** (roughly flat)

**EPS looks stable only because the share count fell from 50M to 45M.** Per-share earnings are
being held up by shrinking the denominator, not by earning more money. Strip out the buyback
and the earnings trend is clearly downward.

A retailer selling more while earning less is the classic signature of **promotional
pressure** — discounting to hold traffic. Operating margin has compressed to **12.4%**.

The screen also reported a **15.0% six-year revenue CAGR**; the recent three-year rate is
**6.7%**, distorted by the depressed pandemic base year.

## 3. Valuation

20.4x earnings and a 4.5% free cash flow yield — the most expensive of the four candidates on
cash flow, and the only one where earnings are actually declining. There is **no valuation
cushion**: if the market stops giving credit for buyback-driven EPS, the multiple has room to
fall a long way.

ROIC of 49.3% is genuinely excellent and reflects a capital-light, high-turnover retail model.
But high ROIC on *shrinking* profits is a description of a good business getting worse, not a
reason to buy.

That **+1.6%** expected return is the lowest of the four by a wide margin, and the 35%
probability of loss is the highest.

**Bear:** comparable sales decelerate through the guided 2–3% second half and turn negative;
operating margin keeps compressing; the multiple de-rates once buybacks can no longer mask
falling net income.

**Base:** raised guidance holds (6.7–7.2% sales growth, 3.2–3.7% comps, EPS $28.70–29.00);
margin compression flattens rather than reverses; the buyback keeps shrinking the share count
~5% a year.

**Bull:** promotional intensity eases and operating margin recovers toward 14%; comps
re-accelerate above the guided range; the multiple expands.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

**Real and dated — and genuinely positive:**
1. **27 August 2026: management RAISED full-year guidance.** Net sales growth to 6.7–7.2%
   (from 6.0–7.0%), comparable sales to 3.2–3.7% (from 2.5–3.5%), operating income growth to
   8.3–9.3%, diluted EPS to **$28.70–29.00** (growth of 11.9–13.1%).
2. **Q2 beat:** net sales $3.0B (+8.9%), EPS $6.55 against a $6.17 consensus.

**The offsetting facts, which is why this is not enough:**
- The **second-half guide implies a sharp slowdown**: 4–5% sales growth and 2–3% comps.
- **Q2 comps of 3.8% were down from 6.7%** a year earlier — decelerating, not accelerating.
- **Shares fell on the caution despite the raise.** The market read the same thing.
- The guided EPS growth of ~12% sits on top of a ~5% annual share-count reduction, so the
  underlying profit growth implied is far smaller.

**Note on fiscal-year labelling:** Ulta's "fiscal 2026" ends January 2027, while the SEC
period ending January 2026 is the year just reported. Guidance and filed results are one year
apart in labelling. Comparing them naively is an easy and serious error.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: the margin compression is structural, not cyclical.** Three
consecutive years of falling operating income during a period of *rising* sales is not noise.
Something is costing Ulta more to sell each dollar of product.

**Competitive pressure is the likely cause.** Sephora's expansion inside Kohl's put prestige
beauty into a mass channel. Amazon has pushed hard into beauty. Brands increasingly sell
direct to consumers, bypassing retailers entirely. Beauty retail has **low switching costs** —
a loyalty programme is a nudge, not a moat.

**The buyback is flattering the story.** Management is not doing anything improper — buying
back stock is legitimate capital allocation. But headline EPS guidance of "+11.9–13.1%
growth" describes per-share arithmetic, not business performance, and the distinction is not
being foregrounded. An investor reading only the EPS line would not know net income fell 11%
over three years.

**No valuation cushion.** At 20.4x earnings with a 4.5% FCF yield, this is priced as a healthy
grower. It is not currently growing profits at all.

**Consumer discretionary cyclicality.** Beauty is more resilient than most discretionary
retail (the "lipstick effect"), but a genuine consumer downturn still hurts a $549 stock
trading on optimism about a second-half re-acceleration.

**What would prove me wrong:** two consecutive quarters of *expanding* operating margin, or
net income growth that does not depend on the share count falling.

## 9. Verdict

**AVOID.** The weakest of the four on the measure that matters most: a probability-weighted
return of **+1.6%** with a 35% chance of losing money. You are not being paid to take this
risk.

The balance sheet is pristine and ROIC is excellent, so this is not a troubled company. It is
a good company whose profits are shrinking while its stock is priced as though they are
growing.

**What would change my mind:** evidence that operating margin has stopped compressing — one
quarter of year-over-year operating margin expansion — combined with a lower entry price.
