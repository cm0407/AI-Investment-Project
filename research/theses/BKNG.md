## 1. The business in one paragraph

Booking Holdings runs the world's largest online travel marketplace — Booking.com, Priceline,
Agoda, Kayak and OpenTable. It does not own hotels. It connects travellers to properties and
takes a commission on each booking, which makes it asset-light: it needs very little capital
to grow. Its strongest position is European hotel supply, where its inventory of independent
properties is difficult for a competitor to replicate.

## 3. Valuation

At 25.1x earnings and 14.9x free cash flow, Booking is **not cheap**. This is a
quality-at-a-fair-price idea, not a bargain. The return has to come from earnings growth and
buybacks, not from the multiple expanding.

The buyback is doing real work: the share count fell 20.7% over the window, so per-share
figures grow meaningfully faster than the company does. Q2 2026 alone returned **$4.1B** to
shareholders, **$3.7B of it repurchases — the largest quarterly return in company history.**

**Bear:** consumer recession cuts discretionary travel; room-night growth goes flat or
negative against the guided 3–5%; take rate and marketing efficiency compress *at the same
time* — these risks are correlated, not independent; multiple derates toward 11x FCF.

**Base:** the company delivers its guided high-single-digit growth in gross bookings, revenue
and adjusted EBITDA; adjusted EPS grows low-to-mid teens, roughly half of it from the
buyback; the multiple holds near 25x.

**Bull:** travel demand proves resilient and room nights re-accelerate above 5%; the record
buyback pace continues; the multiple expands on evidence the deceleration was temporary.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

**Real and dated:**
1. **Q3 2026 results.** Management guided room nights +3–5% and gross bookings, revenue and
   adjusted EBITDA each +4–6%. The test is whether the deceleration stops there.
2. **Full-year 2026 guidance** of high-single-digit growth in gross bookings, revenue and
   adjusted EBITDA, with adjusted EPS growth in the **low-to-mid teens**.
3. **The buyback.** Mechanical and enormous — $3.7B in a single quarter against a $135.5B
   market cap.

**Honest framing:** these catalysts point *downward* in rate terms. Guidance is for slower
growth than delivered. This is a decelerating business returning a lot of cash, not an
accelerating one.

**Management-flagged headwind:** the outlook explicitly assumes continued indirect impacts
from the Middle East conflict.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: travel is discretionary and deeply cyclical.** In a consumer
recession, room nights, take rate and marketing efficiency all deteriorate together. The
2020 column above — revenue down 60%, an operating loss — is not a hypothetical. It happened
six years ago.

**The valuation offers no protection.** At 25x earnings, a de-rating to 18x is −28% before
any operating disappointment. Buying a cyclical at a full multiple heading into decelerating
guidance is the classic way to lose money in a good business.

**Negative equity removes a safety check.** Shareholders' equity is **−$5.6B**. Booking has
bought back more stock than it has retained in earnings. This is not distress — cash flow is
strong and net debt/EBITDA is only 0.16 — but it does two things: it makes ROIC
uncomputable, removing a quality check the system would otherwise apply, and it thins the
cushion if a downturn arrives.

**Insider behaviour is one-sided.** Across the Form 4 filings examined: **55 open-market
sales, zero purchases**, netting **−$33.3M**. This proves nothing on its own — most executive
sales run through pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plans and are routine diversification. But not one
insider bought at any price, and that is a fact worth recording rather than explaining away.

**Structural threats to the moat:**
- Google increasingly inserting itself between the traveller and the OTA.
- Hotel chains pushing direct booking to avoid the commission.
- A large share of traffic is *rented* through paid marketing, not owned. That is a moat with
  a monthly bill attached.

**What would prove me wrong:** two consecutive quarters of negative room-night growth, or
evidence that marketing spend as a share of gross bookings is rising to hold volume flat.

## 7. Data caveats

- **Per-share history is not comparable with today's price.** The latest cover-page share
  count (751.4M) is **23.0x** the diluted weighted-average count in the filings (33M),
  indicating a stock split not reflected in the historical per-share series. Filed EPS of
  $165.57 for FY2025 is on the **old** share base and must not be compared with a $180 price.
  Whole-company ratios (P/E from market cap ÷ net income, P/FCF, EV/EBITDA) remain valid
  because both sides scale together. The system now detects this automatically.
- **ROIC is unavailable** because invested capital cannot be computed from negative equity.
  One quality check is therefore simply missing.
- **Tag substitutions:** revenue → `Revenues`; net income →
  `NetIncomeLossAvailableToCommonStockholdersBasic`; also `sga_expense`, `dividends_paid`,
  `stock_comp`, `shares_outstanding`.
- Q2 2026 operating figures come from earnings coverage, not the 10-K.

## 9. Verdict

**WATCH, not BUY.** The highest score of the four, and a genuinely excellent business — but
the score flatters it. Growth is decelerating by management's own guidance, the multiple is
full, insiders are selling only, and the probability-weighted return is **+8.4%** against a
33% downside. That is not the risk/reward profile of a 20%-upside idea.

**What would move this to BUY:** a meaningful de-rating (toward 18–19x earnings) without a
corresponding deterioration in room-night growth — in other words, a price fall driven by
market fear rather than by the business.
