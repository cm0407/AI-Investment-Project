## 1. The business in one paragraph

Amphenol makes **interconnect products** — the connectors, cables and sensors that carry
power and data between components inside electronic equipment. It is a picks-and-shovels
supplier: it does not build AI servers, it sells the parts that plug them together. Its
largest market is **IT datacom** (datacentre equipment), which is currently 43% of sales and
is where the AI buildout is spending.

## 3. Valuation

23.2× earnings does not look expensive for a company that just grew revenue 52%.

**But that multiple is calculated on boom-year earnings, and that is exactly how cyclicals
trap people.** The classic pattern: a cyclical business looks *cheapest* at the top of its
cycle, because trailing earnings are at a peak the market already knows will not last. Buying
a 23× multiple on peak earnings means paying a much higher multiple on normalised earnings.

My own lessons file records this in a different form — the warning about EOG, where a cheap
multiple sat on top of revenue that was actually shrinking. Amphenol is the inverse case:
the multiple is reasonable but the earnings underneath it are elevated.

I cannot tell you where in the AI capex cycle we are. Nobody can. That uncertainty is the
reason this is a WATCH.

**Bear assumptions:**
- AI datacentre capex enters a digestion phase; IT datacom (43% of sales) goes from +89% growth to flat or negative.
- The 23.2× multiple is revealed to sit on peak-cycle earnings, so earnings and multiple compress together.
- Hyperscaler order patterns prove lumpy and one missed quarter resets expectations.
- CommScope integration absorbs management attention during the downturn.

**Base assumptions:**
- AI infrastructure spending continues for another year at a decelerating but still-strong rate.
- Q3 guidance of $9.3–9.4B (50–52% growth) is met, then growth moderates toward 15–20%.
- Operating margin holds near 25% as volume leverage persists.
- Multiple compresses modestly as growth normalises, offsetting part of the earnings growth.

**Bull assumptions:**
- The AI buildout proves longer and broader than expected; optical and power interconnect content per rack keeps rising.
- Amphenol takes share across both high-speed and power interconnect as complexity increases.
- The CommScope acquisition adds accretive interconnect exposure.
- Earnings grow into the multiple rather than the multiple compressing.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

**Real and dated:**
1. **Q3 2026 guidance of $9.3–9.4B revenue, 50–52% year-over-year growth** — a specific,
   public, testable number.
2. **IT datacom grew 89% year over year** and is now 43% of sales.
3. **The CommScope connectivity acquisition** adds interconnect exposure.

**The honest framing:** every catalyst above is a *continuation* catalyst. They all say "the
boom keeps going." None of them is a company-specific event that creates value independent of
the cycle. That is a materially weaker catalyst set than it first appears.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: AI datacentre capex is cyclical, and 43% of sales depend on it.**
Hyperscalers can pause. When they do, orders do not decline gently — they stop, because
Amphenol sits in a supply chain where customers hold inventory. A business growing 52% can go
to 0% in two quarters without anything being wrong with the company.

**Peak-cycle earnings and peak-cycle multiple compress together.** This is the specific
mechanism that makes cyclicals dangerous. If earnings fall 30% and the multiple falls from 23×
to 16×, the stock halves. That is how I arrived at a −38% bear case — it is arithmetic on a
normalisation, not a catastrophe scenario.

**I cannot time the cycle, and I will not pretend to.** My macro note for this session records
credit spreads at 2.71% — historically very tight, meaning the market is pricing very little
risk anywhere. That is the environment in which cycles usually turn, but "usually" is not a
forecast.

**Customer concentration.** A small number of hyperscalers drive the incremental demand. The
filings do not break this out in a way I could verify, which is itself a limitation.

**Share count rose 4.6%**, so some of the earnings growth is being shared with employees
rather than shareholders.

**My own bias to watch:** this company scores well on every quality metric precisely *because*
it is in a boom. The scoring engine measures growth, margins and returns on trailing data —
all of which peak at exactly the wrong moment to buy. A high score on a cyclical at the top of
its cycle is a warning, not an endorsement.

**What would prove me wrong, specifically:** IT datacom growth decelerating below 20% year over
year, or any quarter where book-to-bill falls below 1.0.

## 8. Verdict

**WATCH, and the one I would be slowest to buy.** Amphenol is a genuinely excellent operator —
25.4% operating margins, strong cash conversion, a real position in a growing market. Nothing
here is a criticism of the business.

The problem is entirely about *when*. You would be buying a cyclical at 23× trailing earnings
after a 52% revenue year, with 43% of sales tied to the most talked-about capital spending
boom in the market. The probability-weighted return is **+3.4%** against a **−38%** downside.
That is not adequate compensation.

**What would move this to BUY:** a meaningful price fall (toward $55–60) *without* a
corresponding collapse in datacom orders — that is, fear rather than deterioration. Or
evidence that AI interconnect demand is structural rather than a buildout phase, which would
take several more quarters of data.
