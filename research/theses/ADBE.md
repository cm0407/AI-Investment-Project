## 1. The business in one paragraph

Adobe sells creative and document software by subscription — Photoshop, Illustrator,
Premiere, and the PDF/Acrobat franchise — to professionals, businesses and, increasingly,
casual users. Customers pay monthly or annually, which makes revenue recurring and
predictable. It has three segments: Digital Media (the Creative Cloud and Document Cloud
subscriptions, the large majority of revenue), Digital Experience (marketing and analytics
software sold to enterprises), and Publishing.

## 3. Valuation

**Reverse DCF — what the price already assumes.** At $257.26, with a 10% required return
and 2.5% terminal growth, the market is pricing Adobe for roughly **1–2% annual free cash
flow growth for a decade**. Adobe has delivered **13.2%**. The price embeds near-stagnation.

That is the whole thesis in one line: *you are not paying for growth; you are being paid to
take the risk that growth stops.*

**Multiples.** 14.3x earnings and 10.4x free cash flow for a business with 36.6% operating
margins is a valuation normally attached to a declining industrial, not a software
franchise with net cash.

**Peer sanity check.** Alphabet, also screened, trades at 31.3x earnings with a 36.9%
operating margin — a very similar margin profile at more than double the multiple.

**Bear assumptions:** AI-native tools keep taking the prosumer and small-business flank;
ARR growth decays from 10.9% toward mid-single digits; free-tier shift and AI compute costs
compress the operating margin; the market treats Adobe as a declining franchise and the
multiple stays at 10–12x free cash flow on falling cash flow.

**Base assumptions:** the enterprise and professional core holds; ARR growth stabilises at
8–10%; the $25B buyback keeps shrinking the share count against a depressed price; the
multiple re-rates modestly toward 16–17x earnings as the panic fades.

**Bull assumptions:** Adobe demonstrates AI monetisation that adds revenue rather than
cannibalising it; growth re-accelerates toward 12%+; the multiple returns toward 20x — still
below its own historical average.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

**Real and dated:**
1. **Q3 FY2026 earnings, due within weeks.** The specific number that matters is the ARR
   growth rate — whether it stabilises near 10.9% or decays further.
2. **The $25B buyback authorisation.** Mechanical, not a hope: it shrinks the share count
   while the price is depressed. Share count is already down 12.0% over the window.

**Not a catalyst, though often described as one:** "AI will eventually help Adobe." There is
no date and no measurable trigger attached to it. Per CLAUDE.md Phase 4, that is optimism,
not a catalyst.

**A warning from the record:** Adobe has beaten EPS estimates five straight quarters and
still averages roughly a −5% move on the day. Beating estimates is evidently *not* the
catalyst. The market is repricing the franchise's future, not its next quarter.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: generative AI destroys the moat rather than extending it.**
Adobe's franchise rests on the skilled production of images, layouts and video being hard.
If AI makes that easy, the value migrates from the tool to the model. This is not
speculative — it is the reason the stock fell 37% this year.

**The evidence the bears are right:**
- ARR growth has decelerated to ~10.9%, below the rate that supported the old multiple.
- Canva and Figma are reported growing ARR at roughly 30% and 40% — multiples of Adobe's rate.
  Share is moving, and it is moving in one direction.
- Adobe is pushing free tiers, which is what an incumbent does when defending, not attacking.

**Other ways to lose money here:**
- **Value trap.** Cheap can stay cheap for years. A 14x multiple can become an 11x multiple
  without anything dramatic happening, and that alone is a −23% move.
- **Margin compression from AI costs.** Inference is expensive. Defending with AI features
  given away free is a direct hit to that 36.6% operating margin.
- **Buyback at the wrong price.** Management bought aggressively at roughly double today's
  multiple. That capital is spent and cannot be re-deployed at today's better prices.
- **Leadership and narrative risk.** Reported leadership uncertainty compounds the problem;
  the market is not being given a story it believes.
- **My own bias.** A statistically cheap, high-quality franchise is *exactly* the setup that
  attracts value investors into declining businesses. Kodak was cheap. Nokia was cheap.

**What would prove me wrong, specifically:** two consecutive quarters of ARR growth below
8%, or evidence of net revenue retention falling in the enterprise segment. Either would
mean the erosion has reached the core, and the thesis should be abandoned rather than
averaged down.

## 7. Data caveats

- **ROIC of 63.2% is flattered by convention.** Invested capital excludes cash
  ($11.62B equity + $6.21B debt − $6.59B cash = $11.24B). The business is genuinely
  capital-light, but the headline number overstates it. See `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`.
- **Tag substitutions:** revenue resolves to `Revenues`, not the preferred
  `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax`; `sga_expense`, `long_term_debt`
  and `intangibles` also use fallback tags.
- **Competitor ARR figures for Canva and Figma come from financial journalism**, not
  filings — they are private companies. CLAUDE.md ranks this tier 6 of 7. They are directionally
  informative, not verified.
- Forward guidance figures are management's own projections, not results.

## 9. Verdict

**WATCH, not BUY.** The valuation is genuinely attractive and the quality is real. But 72.7
sits in the "worth monitoring" band, not the "strong" band, and the reason is honest: the
bear case is *structural*, not cyclical. A 25% chance of reaching +20% is not a good enough
hit rate to justify a large position when the downside scenario is a permanent impairment of
the franchise rather than a temporary drawdown.

**What would move this to BUY:** ARR growth stabilising at or above 10% for two consecutive
quarters, or clear evidence of AI-driven revenue expansion in the Digital Media segment.
