## 1. The business in one paragraph

PTC sells engineering software to manufacturers. Two products matter: **CAD**
(computer-aided design — the software engineers draw parts in) and **PLM** (product lifecycle
management — the system of record holding every revision, bill of materials and supplier
detail for a physical product). Customers pay an annual subscription. The key measure is
**ARR**, annual recurring revenue: the yearly run-rate of those subscriptions.

## 3. Valuation

**Reverse DCF.** The price assumes about **8.6% annual free-cash-flow growth**. PTC has
delivered **32.0%**, and is currently guiding ARR growth of 9–9.5%. The bar the market has set
is one the company is already clearing.

**But the ceiling is low, and that is the honest counterweight.** A business growing ARR at 9%
does not re-rate dramatically. The return has to come from cash-flow growth plus buybacks, not
from multiple expansion. This is the lowest-ceiling idea in the session.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

1. **Q3 FY2026 constant-currency ARR of $2.448B, up 9.1%** excluding divestitures — above the
   guided 8–9% range.
2. **Management raised the low end of full-year guidance** to roughly 9–9.5% ARR growth.
3. **Operating and free cash flow both beat the high end of guidance** (Q3 FCF $249M).
4. **$626M of buybacks** executed, shrinking the share count.
5. **Divestiture of Kepware and ThingWorx completed 13 March 2026**, concentrating the company
   on core CAD/PLM.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: this is a bet on manufacturing capex.** PTC's customers are
industrial manufacturers. If they stop funding digital transformation projects, seat growth
stalls and ARR decelerates toward 5–6%. Software is sticky, so revenue would not collapse —
but the growth premium would disappear, and at 19× earnings there is not much cushion.

**The growth rate is the problem, not the price.** At ~9% ARR growth, even a successful year
produces a modest return. A beginner hunting 20% should understand that reaching it here
requires multiple expansion that nothing currently guarantees.

**Divestitures make the reported numbers hard to read.** The most recent complete filed year
(FY2025, ended 30 September 2025) predates the March 2026 sale of Kepware and ThingWorx. The
P/E of 19.0× is computed on a business that no longer has quite the same shape, and fiscal
2026 GAAP figures will contain one-time divestiture gains. **The ARR figure is the cleaner
measure** and is what I have leaned on.

**Competition is well-funded.** Dassault Systèmes, Siemens and Autodesk all target the same
customers. Switching is rare, but competition caps what PTC can charge at renewal.

**Share count rose 3.9%** despite $626M of buybacks, meaning stock compensation is absorbing
most of the repurchase. Shareholders are running to stand still.

**What would prove me wrong, specifically:** two consecutive quarters of constant-currency ARR
growth below 7%, or net new ARR falling year over year.

## 8. Verdict

**WATCH.** The most *reliable* idea in the session and the best-measured — the only company
scoring on all 16 data components. Switching costs are genuinely punitive (migrating a
manufacturer's engineering record means re-validating decades of product data), cash
conversion is excellent, and the market's implied bar of 8.6% sits below what the company is
already delivering.

It is a WATCH rather than a BUY because the upside is structurally capped. My base case is
+18%, and reaching +20% needs the multiple to expand — which I cannot point to a specific
reason for.

**What would move this to BUY:** ARR growth accelerating above 11%, or a price fall toward
$105–110 without a corresponding deterioration in ARR.
