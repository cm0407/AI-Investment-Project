## 1. The business in one paragraph

EOG Resources drills for and sells crude oil and natural gas in the United States, mainly in
the Permian, Eagle Ford and — since the Encino acquisition — the Utica shale. It is an
exploration and production company, which means it sells a **commodity at a price it does not
set**. Its profit depends far more on the world oil price than on anything management does.

### ⚠️ The growth number in the screen was wrong in spirit

The screen ranked EOG #2 partly on a **15.5% six-year revenue CAGR**. That figure is
meaningless. FY2020 was the oil-price crash — the year oil futures briefly went negative.
Compounding out of that trough measures recovery.

- Six-year CAGR: **+15.5%**
- **Recent three-year CAGR: −4.2%**
- **Revenue has declined three consecutive years. Net income has fallen from $7.76B to $5.00B — a 36% decline.**

This is a **shrinking** business by revenue, not a growing one, and the screen presented the
opposite. The system now flags this automatically.

## 3. Valuation

On multiples, EOG looks genuinely cheap: 8.0x free cash flow and a **12.5% free cash flow
yield**, with a conservative balance sheet.

**But a cheap multiple on a commodity producer is a trap in both directions.** E&P companies
look cheapest at the *top* of the cycle, when trailing cash flow is at its peak and the market
is already pricing the decline. A 12.5% FCF yield is only real if the oil price that produced
it persists.

That is the core problem with valuing this company: **the answer is a function of an oil price
forecast, and I do not have one.** Any DCF I produce would be a disguised oil-price bet.

synergy target; the multiple expands from 7.4x EV/EBITDA.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

**Real and dated:**
1. **2026 capital plan:** targets ~**$4.5B free cash flow** at strip pricing, with **5% oil
   production growth and 13% total production growth** including Encino.
2. **Capital return.** EOG returned **100% of 2025 free cash flow** to shareholders — an 8%
   dividend increase plus $2.5B of buybacks. Q2 2026 returned $1.8B ($540M dividends, $1.3B
   repurchases). This is mechanical, not a hope.
3. **Encino integration** exceeded its **$150M synergy target**; the acquisition added roughly
   10% to oil production.

**The dominant variable is not a catalyst.** Everything above is swamped by the oil price,
which has no schedule and which no one reliably forecasts. Listing production growth as a
catalyst while ignoring that revenue fell during a period of production growth would be
misleading.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: this is fundamentally a bet on the oil price, and I cannot make
that bet responsibly.** Everything else — operational quality, balance sheet, capital return —
is real but secondary. In a downcycle, a well-run E&P still loses a great deal of money.

**Revenue is already falling.** Three consecutive years of decline, and net income down 36%
from 2022, *before* any recession. Production grew while revenue shrank, which tells you
price is doing the damage.

**The risks are correlated, not diversified.** A falling oil price simultaneously cuts
revenue, free cash flow, the dividend's headroom and the buyback. There is no offsetting leg.

**Pro-cyclical capital allocation.** Returning 100% of free cash flow sounds shareholder-
friendly, and directionally it is — but it means buying back the most stock when cash flows
and prices are highest, and the least when the stock is cheapest. That is backwards, and it is
structural to the industry.

**Depleting assets.** An E&P must keep spending simply to stand still. Reserves deplete;
capital expenditure is not optional.

**It conflicts with the user's stated goal.** The preference file targets ~20% returns over
about a year. A commodity producer's 12-month return is close to a coin flip on a
macroeconomic variable, which is a poor fit for a beginner's satellite sleeve regardless of
company quality.

## 9. Verdict

**AVOID.** At 67.6 this sits in the speculative band, and the score is *generous* — the growth
pillar earned 12.0/15 on a CAGR that inverts the truth about the business. Corrected for
that, EOG scores below 65.

This is a well-run company at a cheap multiple whose 12-month return depends chiefly on a
variable I cannot forecast. The probability-weighted return of +7.0% does not compensate for
a −32% bear case.

**What would change my mind:** not a lower price. A framework for forecasting oil that I do
not have, or a price so distressed that the downside is priced in even at $50 oil.
