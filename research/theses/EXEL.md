## 1. The business in one paragraph

Exelixis is an oncology company. Almost all of its revenue comes from one drug family,
**cabozantinib** (sold as CABOMETYX), used mainly in kidney and liver cancer. Its future rests
on a successor compound, **zanzalintinib**, currently under review by the FDA. A drug
company's moat is its patent — a legal monopoly that is powerful while it lasts and worth
nothing afterwards.

## 3. Valuation

**Reverse DCF.** The price assumes about **6.8% annual free-cash-flow growth** against
**37.4% delivered**. On its face that is a wide gap — but it exists for a reason the market
understands perfectly well: cabozantinib's patents wind down, so the market is refusing to
extrapolate current cash flow. A cheap multiple on a drug approaching patent expiry is not
automatically a bargain.

**The real question is not the multiple — it is the December date.** If zanzalintinib is
approved, Exelixis becomes a two-franchise company and 18.2× looks clearly too cheap. If it is
not, Exelixis is a single-asset company with a visible expiry, and 18.2× may be about right.

Critically, management's 2026 revenue guidance of **$2.53–2.63B explicitly excludes any
zanzalintinib revenue.** A positive decision is upside to the guided numbers, not already in
them.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

1. **FDA target action date of 3 December 2026** for zanzalintinib in previously treated
   metastatic colorectal cancer — the single best-dated catalyst found in this session,
   roughly three months away, with a binary and knowable outcome.
2. **2026 revenue guidance of $2.53–2.63B excludes zanzalintinib entirely**, so approval is
   upside to the guided figures.
3. **Cabozantinib generated $573.0M of U.S. net product revenue in Q2 2026** — the base
   business funding everything.
4. **Broad pivotal programme** behind it in renal, neuroendocrine and other tumour types.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: the FDA says no on 3 December.** This is the most concentrated
single-event risk in the session. A rejection or delay removes the second franchise from the
story and leaves a single-drug company facing patent erosion. **A beginner should understand
this clearly: a meaningful part of the outcome resolves on one day, on a decision nobody
outside the FDA controls.**

**One drug is the whole company.** Essentially all revenue is cabozantinib. There is no second
product cushioning a setback. Sales are projected to peak around $2.8B by 2029 before generic
competition erodes them.

**The moat is a wasting asset.** Unlike a brand or a network, a patent has an expiry date
printed on it. Every year that passes without a successor makes the company worth less,
independent of how well management executes.

**Approval is not the same as commercial success.** Even a December approval merely starts a
launch — building prescriber adoption and payer coverage takes quarters, and first-year
revenue is usually modest. The stock could get its approval and still disappoint.

**Biotech is outside my circle of competence.** I cannot evaluate the clinical data behind
zanzalintinib. I am reasoning from financial statements and regulatory timelines, not from
oncology. That is a real limitation on how much weight this thesis deserves, and I would
rather state it than imply an expertise I do not have.

**What would prove me wrong, specifically:** an FDA complete response letter or a delay to the
3 December action date; or any indication that cabozantinib erosion is starting earlier than
the 2029 projection.

## 8. Verdict

**WATCH — and the most speculative idea presented.** It earns its place on the strength of one
thing: a hard, dated, high-impact catalyst that is explicitly excluded from guidance. That is
rare and genuinely valuable.

But the catalyst is binary, the company is essentially one drug, and I cannot assess the
clinical evidence. The 76.7 score reflects strong financials (45.7 of 50 data points) pulled
down by a moat score of 4/10 and risk/reward of 5/10.

**For a beginner specifically:** this is the one idea here where a bad outcome arrives
suddenly rather than gradually. Holding it into 3 December means being prepared for a sharp
move in either direction on a single day.

**What would move this to BUY:** approval on 3 December plus early evidence of prescriber
uptake.
