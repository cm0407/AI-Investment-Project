## 1. The business in one paragraph

Intuit sells financial software by subscription. Three things matter: **TurboTax** (consumer
tax filing), **QuickBooks** (small-business accounting, plus payroll and payments), and
**Credit Karma / Mailchimp**. Customers pay every year, which makes revenue recurring. The
QuickBooks franchise is the more valuable half — once a business keeps its books, payroll and
payments in one system, and its accountant works in that system too, moving is painful.

## 3. Valuation

**Reverse DCF — the heart of the thesis.** At $312.77, with a 10% required return and 2.5%
terminal growth, the market is pricing Intuit for free cash flow to **shrink about 5.9% a
year for a decade.**

Intuit has grown free cash flow at **22.1% a year**.

The price does not merely assume growth stops. It assumes the business goes into permanent
decline. That is the bet on offer: you are being *paid* to take AI risk, rather than paying up
for growth.

**Multiples.** 18.3× earnings against a four-year historical average above 30×. A 10.4% free
cash flow yield on $8.66B of actual cash.

**Peer check.** Adobe — analysed in the previous session with a very similar AI-disruption
narrative — trades at 14.3× with ARR growth decelerating to ~10.9%. Intuit is modestly more
expensive but is still growing revenue **14%**, which is the material difference between them.

**Bear:** AI genuinely commoditises bookkeeping and guided tax prep; QuickBooks retention
slips, not just TurboTax pricing; TurboTax DIY unit growth of 2–3% turns negative; the 17%
workforce cut proves to be a response to demand weakness rather than a margin choice; the
multiple stays at 14–15× on flat-to-falling cash flow.

**Base:** the consumer DIY franchise stabilises at low single-digit growth while Big Bets
(34% growth, now 30% of revenue) carry the company; mid-market and money segments keep growing
above 30%; the headcount reduction expands margins in FY2027; the multiple re-rates toward
22–23×, still far below the historical average.

**Bull:** AI expands Intuit's addressable work rather than replacing it; TurboTax Live keeps
growing customers 38% and revenue 36%; free cash flow compounds toward $10B+; the multiple
returns toward 28–30×.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

**Real and dated:**
1. **FY2027 guidance is already set** — the bar is public and testable.
2. **The 17% workforce reduction** ($300–340M restructuring charge) flows mechanically into
   FY2027 margins whether or not anything else improves.
3. **Big Bets grew 34% and are now 30% of revenue** — a measurable mix shift toward the
   faster-growing, less AI-exposed segments, reported every quarter.
4. **Multi-year Anthropic partnership** putting AI agents into the mid-market platform.

**Not a catalyst:** "AI will eventually help Intuit." No date, no measurable trigger.

**A warning from the record:** Intuit was *"absolutely hammered after a beat-and-raise
quarter."* Beating estimates is evidently not the catalyst — the market is repricing the
franchise's future, not its next quarter. Exactly the pattern Adobe shows.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: AI erodes QuickBooks, not just TurboTax.** TurboTax weakness is
already visible and partly priced. The larger danger is the small-business accounting
franchise — if AI agents can keep books competently, the switching costs that protect
QuickBooks weaken from both ends.

**The evidence the bears already have:**
- Intuit **lowered its own fiscal 2026 TurboTax revenue forecast.**
- **TurboTax unit growth is guided to just 2–3%.**
- The **CEO conceded** the company *"lost quality DIY customers to low-cost providers this
  year"* over pricing. That is not an analyst's speculation; it is management's own admission.
- A **17% workforce reduction** is a large correction to make in one step.
- The stock is down ~38% year to date, with a maximum drawdown near 61% from its peak. The
  market has had many months to think about this and has kept selling.

**Other ways to lose money here:**
- **Value trap.** An 18× multiple can become a 14× multiple without anything dramatic
  happening — that alone is −23%.
- **No buyback support.** Share count is roughly flat (+1.5%). Management is not aggressively
  repurchasing at a price they presumably consider cheap, which is a mild negative signal.
- **Pricing power is the thing in question.** The DIY loss was a *price* problem. If Intuit
  cannot raise prices without losing customers, the margin story weakens.
- **My own bias.** A statistically cheap, high-quality franchise under a technology cloud is
  precisely the setup that has lured value investors into declining businesses for decades.
  I said the same thing about Adobe two sessions ago and it remains unresolved.
- **Concentration risk in my own conclusions.** Intuit and Adobe are the same bet wearing
  different clothes: *"the market is wrong about AI destroying incumbent software."* If that
  call is wrong, it is wrong for both.

**What would prove me wrong, specifically:** two consecutive quarters of QuickBooks /
Online Ecosystem revenue growth below 10%, or any decline in small-business subscriber
counts. Either would mean the erosion has reached the durable half of the company.

## 9. Verdict

**WATCH — and it lands 0.6 points below the BUY threshold.**

I want to be explicit about that, because it would have been easy to move one judgment score
by a single point and call this a BUY. The catalysts pillar is where the temptation sat: I
scored Intuit 6/10, the same as Adobe, because in both cases the catalyst that would actually
re-rate the stock — proof that AI is not eating the franchise — has no date attached. Scoring
Intuit 7 while scoring Adobe 6 for an equivalent situation would have been inflation dressed
as judgment.

The genuine positives are unusual: the cleanest growth record in the screen, a 10.4% free cash
flow yield, and a price that assumes permanent decline in a business growing revenue 14%.

The genuine negative is that management has *confirmed* the bear case's first premise — they
lost customers on price this year. The question is whether that was a one-off error or the
first visible crack.

**What would move this to BUY:** QuickBooks/Online Ecosystem growth holding above 10% for two
quarters, or evidence of a meaningful buyback at these prices.
