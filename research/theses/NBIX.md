## 1. The business in one paragraph

Neurocrine develops and sells drugs for neurological and endocrine conditions. Two products
matter. **INGREZZA** treats tardive dyskinesia — involuntary movements caused by long-term use
of certain psychiatric medicines — and is the larger of the two. **CRENESSITY**, approved in
December 2024, treats congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH), a rare inherited hormone disorder,
and currently has that indication effectively to itself.

## 3. Valuation

32.2× earnings is not cheap, and I want to be direct about that.

**The reverse DCF is why it still qualifies.** At $151.83, with a 10% required return, the
market is pricing Neurocrine for about **12.7% annual free-cash-flow growth**. It has delivered
**28.0%**. The bar is roughly half what the company has achieved, and Q1 2026 came in above the
trend rather than below it.

That is the whole argument: a high multiple attached to a low implied growth rate is a
different thing from a high multiple attached to a high implied growth rate. Ubiquiti — rejected
this session — trades at 37.6× with the price demanding **29.5%** growth against **8.9%**
delivered. Same superficial "expensive"; opposite situation underneath.

- INGREZZA pricing or reimbursement pressure bites; it still carries the majority of revenue, so a shock there hits everything at once.
- CRENESSITY launch decelerates as the initial diagnosed-patient backlog is worked through.
- Crinetics' atumelnant advances faster than expected toward the CAH indication.
- Multiple derates from 32× on any sign the growth rate is normalising.

**Base assumptions:**
- Combined product sales keep compounding near the 44% pace seen in Q1 2026, decelerating gradually.
- CRENESSITY continues scaling into its monopoly indication without near-term competition.
- Operating margin keeps improving from 21.6% as fixed costs leverage.
- Multiple holds near 32×; return comes from earnings growth, not re-rating.

**Bull assumptions:**
- A Phase 3 readout (osavampator in major depression, or direclidine in schizophrenia) succeeds and adds a credible third franchise.
- CRENESSITY exceeds expectations and the market begins valuing it as a multi-product specialty pharma company.
- Operating margin expands toward the high 20s on scale.
- Multiple expands as concentration risk in INGREZZA visibly declines.

## 5. Catalysts (Phase 4)

**Real, and already visible in reported results:**
1. **Q1 2026 net product sales of $811M, +44% year over year** — growth that is happening, not
   forecast.
2. **CRENESSITY scaling into a monopoly indication.** Approved December 2024, no direct
   competitor in CAH today.
3. **Phase 3 pipeline:** osavampator in major depressive disorder, direclidine in schizophrenia.
   Either would add a third franchise and materially reduce concentration risk.

**Honestly scored 7/10, not higher.** None of the pipeline readouts has a hard public date I
could verify — unlike Exelixis, where the FDA action date of 3 December 2026 is fixed and
knowable. "A Phase 3 result is coming" without a date is weaker than it sounds.

## 6. Why this investment could fail (Phase 5)

**The thesis-breaking risk: INGREZZA still carries most of the revenue.** Every discussion of
CRENESSITY's promise is a discussion about the smaller half of the company. A pricing,
reimbursement or competitive shock to INGREZZA hits the majority of revenue at once, and no
amount of CRENESSITY growth offsets it quickly.

**The moat has a known expiry.** A pharmaceutical patent is a legal monopoly — powerful while
it lasts, worth nothing afterwards. CRENESSITY's monopoly is real *today*; Crinetics'
atumelnant is a credible competitor within roughly three years. I am underwriting a window,
not a franchise.

**Shareholders are being diluted.** Share count rose **4.8%** over the window. This is a
profitable company that could be repurchasing stock and is not, so employee compensation is
being paid partly out of your ownership. Compare Qualys — rejected this session on other
grounds — which cut its share count 10.5% at a similar scale.

**Pricing pressure is a live policy question,** not a hypothetical. U.S. drug pricing
legislation and payer pushback both target exactly this kind of high-priced specialty product.

**32× leaves no room for a stumble.** If growth normalises to 15%, the multiple does not stay
at 32×. Earnings disappointment and multiple compression would arrive together — that is how a
−30% bear case happens without anything catastrophic.

**I cannot evaluate the clinical evidence.** As with Exelixis, I am reasoning from financial
statements, regulatory status and competitive news — not from neurology or endocrinology. That
is a real limit on how much this opinion is worth, and I would rather say so than imply
expertise I do not have.

**What would prove me wrong, specifically:** two consecutive quarters of INGREZZA revenue
declining year over year, or CRENESSITY sales growth falling below 20% before atumelnant
reaches the market.

## 8. Verdict

**WATCH.** The only new candidate of this session to clear the 70 threshold, out of 4,354
companies screened.

It qualifies on one clear argument: growth that is real, accelerating and already reported,
against a price whose implied expectation (12.7%) sits well below what has been delivered
(28.0%). It is held back by genuine concentration in INGREZZA, a moat that expires, and
dilution the company has chosen not to offset.

**Note on your portfolio:** you already have Exelixis open from 11 September. Both are
single-indication-dependent pharmaceutical companies whose value turns on patents and
regulatory outcomes. Holding both would concentrate your sleeve in one kind of risk. Your
stated limit is 40% per sector.

**What would move this to BUY:** a successful Phase 3 readout that credibly adds a third
franchise, or evidence that CRENESSITY has overtaken INGREZZA as the growth engine — either
would break the concentration problem that is currently capping the score.
